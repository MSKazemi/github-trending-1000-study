## What the tests can and cannot establish

The fictional three-case batch intentionally includes a positive actionability code, a **negative** actionability code, a withdrawn denominator-only row, a not-due follow-up, a missing due follow-up and unknown time entries. The regression tests check exact expected coded counts, non-missing and missing tally consistency and absence of raw case IDs in the output. This establishes **software behavior only**—not the truth of participants, their consent, reviewer independence, or outcome status.

A real future pilot report needs a prospectively approved frame and source provenance, participant withdrawals/censoring, category-specific effort measurement calibration, independent rater disagreement/adjudication, a negative/null results ledger, 30/90-day eligibility and attrition accounting, a disclosure-risk review and documented methods approval. No confidence interval, population rate, significance claim, environmental savings figure, or quantified causal effect is justified today.

## Open gate, decision and next action

**Issue #24 disposition: KEEP OPEN.** The user requested complete execution, but real opt-in participation and independent approvals cannot be generated by an AI/CI runner. An authorized study owner and qualified ethics/privacy/methods reviewers must first decide whether, when and under what safe scope a live pilot can proceed. **Do not** auto-close on merging a code-only PR; if GitHub does auto-close, reopen with this evidence and an honest blocker.

Practical lessons for other repositories: freeze schemas and task inclusion before measuring outcomes, retain missing and negative denominators, separate upstream from downstream work, protect participant consent from self-attestation, and use publicly committed **fictional** examples instead of putting private raw study logs in a repository.
