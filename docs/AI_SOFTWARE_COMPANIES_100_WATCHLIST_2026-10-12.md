# 100-company watchlist: AI-native programming, agents and reusable software

**Snapshot date:** 2026-10-12. **Curatorial status:** exploratory **non-systematic, non-ranked industry watchlist**, not a sampled observational research cohort and not empirical evidence of vendor success. **Count:** 100 named companies, brands or operating company-origin organizations across 10 areas. **Machine-readable register:** [`references/ai_software_companies_100_2026-10-12.csv`](../references/ai_software_companies_100_2026-10-12.csv).

## Why this list exists

The [2027–2031 research mission](AI_ERA_CODING_REUSE_RESEARCH_MISSION.md) asks what helps developers and agents **find, independently verify, reuse and maintain existing software** rather than needlessly recreate it. The company list maps the ecosystem that may change those behaviors: model providers, coding agents, repositories, builders, frameworks, sandboxes, evaluations, inference, retrieval, enterprise integrations, and software security.

This watchlist is deliberately broader than GitHub Trending and has **no claim** to represent all companies, all successful start-ups, a market-share ranking, an investment ranking, unbiased sampling, or energy-savings evidence. Several entries are subsidiary brands or historically independent startups now owned by larger companies; **100 entries is not 100 independently owned corporations**. A model/API or commercial service is not necessarily open source; a company that publishes open tools may also sell proprietary products.

**Selection logic (editorial, not scored):** current product relevance to the software/agent pipeline; coverage of leaders and new entrants; relevance to 2027 researcher/developer decisions; official discoverable product or company source; deliberate diversity across ten stack layers. Inclusion is not a certification or endorsement. No venture funding, valuation, downloads, installations, customers, benchmark wins, environmental savings, or revenue claims are inferred from inclusion.

**Source status:** the URL on every row is a **vendor-controlled primary link** for product identification, *not independent validation* of performance or financial success. Selected dated independent or official corroboration appears below. Individual sites and products can change; recheck before quoting in publications. The snapshot did not conduct direct company interviews, audits of all 100 websites, independent financial due diligence, or product benchmark runs.

## What is and is not supported by external evidence

The [Stack Overflow 2026 named-tool question](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data) has **12,255 respondents** and reports (multiple responses allowed) Claude Code **65.5%**, GitHub Copilot **58.7%**, Codex **29.5%**, Cursor **25.5%**. [Survey methodology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/methodology) establishes a nonprobability sample. These figures are **not** global coding-agent market share, company success rates, active accounts, or independent verified reuse.

The [2026 developer technology survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/technology/data) also reports from a **different question with n=13,648** that respondents had experienced plausible-but-wrong code (69.3%), hard-to-review generated code (54.2%) and hallucinated packages/config (45.1%). These are respondent-selected difficulties, not incident rates per software task. They motivate evaluation of correctness, review and dependency validation, *not a quantitative effect estimate for any listed company*.

The [2025 GitHub Octoverse](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/) can inform platform chronology, but AI-related repository counts and stars cannot establish AI authorship, reuse, acceptance, quality or sustainability. See [main study methods](AI_ERA_CODING_REUSE_RESEARCH_MISSION.md) and [evidence-to-lesson grading](EVIDENCE_TO_LESSON_PROTOCOL.md).

## Research questions for company/product comparison

| Theme | Testable question | Necessary observation | Threat to validity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finding existing code | Does structured repository/code search find acceptable existing components more often than default searches or unguided agents? | Frozen independently sampled tasks, pooled candidate judgments and incomplete-recall caveat | Search engines return different candidates; never equate not-found with absent |
| Human vs agent workflows | Do agents raise independently accepted task throughput after review and maintenance? | Task-stratified prospective study, blind tests, all attempted tasks and human time | Confounding and selective deployment of agents |
| Open interoperability | Does model/harness portability reduce switching and upkeep cost? | Same tasks across versions with documented APIs, pinned provider settings and changing prices | Versions and provider costs change rapidly |
| Trust and correct reuse | Do provenance/license/vulnerability gates reduce unsafe reuse without unreasonable time cost? | Versioned dependency artifacts, acceptance/security outcomes, verified false positives | Tool-reported risks are not adjudicated true incidents |
| Useful adoption | Do onboarding, documented APIs and reproducible examples increase external accepted integrations? | Verified downstream use and developer-task outcomes, not just stars | Advertising, popularity and package search ranking |
| Lifecycle effects | Under which comparable tasks, usage volumes and maintenance horizons does reuse reduce measured total impact? | Functional-equivalence gate; metered or bounded kWh, human effort, upstream maintenance, CI and runtime | Rebound, shifted workloads and missing provider power data |

## Suggested first interview/product-case shortlist (not a popularity ranking)

Start with contrasting cases that map *directly* to reuse-first development: **GitHub, Sourcegraph, OpenHands, Cline, Qodo, LangChain, LlamaIndex, Hugging Face, Composio, n8n, Snyk, Chainguard, Langfuse, Cognition, Amp, Cursor, OpenAI, Anthropic, Replit and Factory.**

This is an **intentional case-study frame**, not the 1,000-repo cohort. Predefine comparison tasks *before* picking outcomes, document access/price/license constraints, and include competing quiet or legacy solutions. Avoid turning the presence of a company in this list into a study treatment variable or into a success label.

## Practical lessons for the maintainer's GitHub repositories (hypotheses to test)

1. **Advertise the job, not the amount of generated code:** in each README, specify the independently reproducible task, intended audience and explicit boundaries. **Test:** first-run task success by independent participants.
2. **Make existing implementation discoverable:** publish searchable API/component names, package metadata, examples, version requirements and alternative-tool compatibility. **Test:** task-to-repo retrieval recall@k under a qualified candidate pool.
3. **Give agents a narrow, reviewable contract:** provide AGENTS.md/CONTRIBUTING with test commands, file edit boundaries, network and credential restrictions, provenance and required human review. **Test:** accepted changes and reviewer minutes, including failures.
4. **Ship a real reuse path:** packaged release, semver, license/SPDX, example integration, meaningful compatibility guarantees, changelog and migration guide. **Test:** verified external integration and 90-day successful upgrade.
5. **Use a reuse-first gate:** compare existing packages and prior projects on suitability, security, license, maintenance and integration costs before creating a new repo. **Test:** preregistered reuse-first versus ordinary workflow tasks with acceptability constraints.
6. **Measure externalities:** human review/support load, inference/model costs, tests/CI, energy if actually metered, and upstream maintainer work; **do not** equate LOC or tokens with carbon.
7. **Build evidence, not social proof:** document downstream use with consent, independently executed examples and disconfirming/negative cases. Track stars separately as attention, not utility.
8. **Check the company map quarterly:** record owner/product changes, discontinued APIs, prices and licenses with old snapshots preserved. Do not overwrite earlier facts silently.
