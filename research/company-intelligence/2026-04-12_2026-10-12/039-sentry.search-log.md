### Inclusion, exclusion and data-validity decisions

- **Included = 27 ledger rows.** By segment: Apr 12–30: 4; May: 7; Jun: 4; Jul: 2; Aug: 3; Sep: 4; Oct 1–12: 3. Rows are **not** independent confirmations of company success. Most are first-party changelog entries (E2 documentary) or press releases (E1).
- **Duplicate event groups:** `039-seer-agent` (Apr 28 launch; Sep 29 update), `039-seer-copilot` (May 14 beta; Jun 30 availability), `039-monitors-alerts` (Apr 20 early access; May 7 GA), `039-agent-tracing` (Sep 11 GA; Oct 9 JS update). The press-release/changelog and blog/changelog pairs are each recorded as **one** row.
- **Availability class:** Seer Agent **beta** throughout the window; Copilot handoff **beta → available**; Monitors & Alerts **early access → GA**; Low-Value Spans **beta**; TLS CA change **announced only**; Codecov setting **removed** (reversed/cancelled). A GA label was never back-dated onto a beta.
- **Security classes:** the three advisories are vendor-issued disclosures, not sampled incidents. No in-window breach or exploitation report about Sentry was found by these queries. That is **unknown**, not proven absent.
- **Primary vs independent:** apart from the third-party AI digest (contrary) and the GitLab/OSV advisory databases (which mirror vendor advisories), there were no in-window independent sources. E3/E4 evidence of fix quality, adoption or energy is **absent**.
- **Access:** public pages only. No login, paywall bypass, account creation, private repository, scraping of github.com/trending, exploitation or participant data. Public pages may change after the access date.

A future independent reviewer should re-run these queries, record result totals with UTC timestamps, open the excluded title-only changelog entries, read the blog archive before August 2026 by another route, and spot-check every advisory date and version range before any issue closure.
