## Exclusion, deduplication and date corrections

1. [AI coding hook company notice, 2026-04-10](https://docs.gitguardian.com/releases/saas/2026/04/10/changelog) and the ggshield v1.49.0 March 31 changelog are **baseline only**; neither is in-window product-launch evidence.
2. [Generic detector July 8 page](https://docs.gitguardian.com/releases/saas/2026/07/08/changelog) displays *page publication July 7* and *release July 8*. Both retained; do not silently collapse these dates.
3. [September 24 ggshield v1.55.0 changelog](https://github.com/GitGuardian/ggshield/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) and [September 25 SaaS article](https://docs.gitguardian.com/releases/saas/2026/09/25/changelog) overlap in the underlying tool-output intervention; the event ledger gives them one shared duplicate group. This is two documents, not two independent performance tests.
4. The [Oct 7 GhostAction retrospective](https://blog.gitguardian.com/ghostaction-github-actions-supply-chain-attack-returns/) later displayed updates dated **Oct 13**, past the study cutoff. Post-cutoff statistics/content may not be retroactively represented as Oct 7-known. It was **not** minted as a standalone in-window record here.
5. The [September 22 GitHub App key report](https://blog.gitguardian.com/github-app-private-keys-leaked/) includes **2020–2025 underlying historical leak examples**. The report's **2026 publication** is in scope; the original leak dates are not new 2026 incidents.
6. Vendor released-vs-shipped labels mean vendor attestation or dated artifacts, **not locally executed product functionality**. For public OSS changelog headings, initial publication timestamps are not preserved; event-version dates are recorded and publication-date fields left unknown.
